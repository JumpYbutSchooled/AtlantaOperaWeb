About

The Company

Founded in 1979, The Atlanta Opera celebrates the art of opera in Atlanta, Georgia, continuing a long tradition of community dedication to the ultimate musical form. Reflecting the vitality and innovative spirit of our city, The Atlanta Opera has evolved into a cultural powerhouse.

Under the leadership of internationally recognized stage director and Carl W. Knobloch, Jr. General & Artistic Director Tomer Zvulun since 2014, The Atlanta Opera presents four mainstage productions at Cobb Energy Centre and two innovative Discoveries operas creatively offered in venues throughout metro Atlanta.

If success is rooted in quality—staging world-class productions of timeless classics and cutting-edge stories from modern history—our growth is rooted in courage.

One of the few companies operating during the 2020 pandemic lockdown, The Atlanta Opera presented a full season in “The Big Tent Series,” performing under a circus tent in outdoor settings, fully compliant with health and safety protocols. The crisis also launched The Atlanta Opera Film Studio, resulting in the production and international distribution of livestream opera performances and cinematic operas that meet the film industry’s highest standards. Distribution of the films and a library of entertaining bonus materials have expanded the reach and impact of The Atlanta Opera internationally.

The Atlanta Opera’s education and engagement activities reach students and adults throughout Georgia and the Southeast with an ever-growing impact. In addition, the 96-Hour Opera Project and Festival advances the industry by creating opportunities for under-recognized composers and librettists to advance their careers in opera. The Studio Artists program is making an impact by training the rising generation of artists.

With every production that originates with The Atlanta Opera, we expand our business by leasing sets, scenery, costumes, projections, and custom properties to companies across the country making Atlanta a national hub for opera. The first original Ring cycle produced in the U.S. since the pandemic was launched in 2024 draws the world to Atlanta making this a destination for opera.

With the recent success of a comprehensive funding campaign and the launch of the 45th Season, The Atlanta Opera celebrates achieving a place among the top 10 opera companies in the United States—joining Chicago, Houston, Washington, D.C., and Los Angeles, among others.

Quality, Vitality, Creativity, Diversity, Courage—The Atlanta Opera is in sync with our city.

Our History

Atlanta’s taste for opera began in the early years of the 1900’s with the Metropolitan Opera’s annual week-long tour to Atlanta. Performing at the Auditorium-Armory, the fabulous Fox Theatre and finally the Boisfeuillet Jones Atlanta Civic Center, the Metropolitan Opera’s annual tour was a major social event. Every night of the week featured the performance of a different opera starring opera legends including Enrico Caruso, Leontyne Price, and later, Luciano Pavarotti. The audience included a who’s who of Atlantans and was celebrated with posh parties at the Georgian Terrace, Piedmont Driving Club and other upscale venues.

In 1979, the Atlanta Civic Opera was born, a result of a merger between two competing entities, Atlanta Lyric Opera and Georgia Opera. The first artistic director was noted composer Thomas Pasatieri. The company’s first production was The Seagull on Friday, March 14, 1980 at the Fox Theatre. The following December, a festive gala was held in Symphony Hall with such noted young artists as Catherine Malfitano, Jerry Hadley and Samuel Ramey. In 1985, the company was renamed to The Atlanta Opera.

The Molly Blank Center

The Atlanta Opera is building a permanent home: the Molly Blank Center for Opera and the Arts, in the restored 1941 Bobby Jones Clubhouse on the Atlanta Beltline. Designed by Allen Post of Post Loyal, it will have two 200-seat venues (Rosemary Hall and the Atlanta Opera Coca-Cola Theatre) plus rehearsal, education and office space, set in about 4.7 acres of green space. The project is supported by a $27.5 million gift from The Arthur M. Blank Foundation. Site work began in February 2026, with completion expected in fall 2027.

The NOW Festival

The NOW Festival showcases new opera by composers and librettists from historically under-represented communities. Its centerpiece is the 96-Hour Opera Project, where teams get four days in Atlanta to stage brand-new ten-minute operas and compete for the Antinori Grand Prize: $10,000 and a commission for a new work. The 2025 winner, Rose, Tree by Dina Pruzhansky and Hai-Ting Chinn, has grown into Rosenbaum and Li, a chamber opera about identity, family, and choosing love in a divided world. It premieres at the 2027 festival, June 5–13 at Morehouse College.

The Film Studio

Started during the 2020 shutdown, The Atlanta Opera Film Studio turns the company’s productions into films and livestreams that can be watched anywhere, including its Ring operas Das Rheingold, Die Walküre and Siegfried. They stream on the Film Studio’s website.

The Ring, 2029

Since 2023 the company has been building Wagner’s complete four-opera Ring cycle, the first new Ring made in the U.S. since the pandemic and the first ever staged by a resident company in the Southeast. In June 2029 all four operas will be performed together, twice, at the Ring Festival. See the Season page for dates.

Our Company Culture

At the Atlanta Opera, we are reimagining opera and building the major international opera company that Atlanta deserves. Our artform is rich with cultural relevance and historical context, but it must live and breathe for today. The harmonies and vibrations we create are incomplete without the many voices of our community, and the stories we tell are superficial without the context of diverse experiences. To breathe, our artform requires the inclusion of these voices and experiences, free from discrimination and racism. Opera must be living art that expresses our multifaceted experiences. We are dedicated to celebrating the rich demographic tapestry of Atlanta by including its many diverse voices and experiences in all facets of our organization.

The Atlanta Opera respects all people and passionately celebrates the cultural differences that make this country, our city, and this art form strong. We are committed to fostering community dialogue that celebrates our spectrum of diversity, challenges outmoded world views and encourages thoughtful reflection.

The human condition is timeless and transcendent. It is the life breath of performing arts and only through embracing diverse voices and experiences can we continue to explore the historical context and evolving relevance of existing works today. We will continue to collaborate with our community to contextualize and interpret historical masterpieces while fostering the creation of new ones.
